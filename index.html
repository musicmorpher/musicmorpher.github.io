MusicMorpher

Music Morphing via Style Tokens

Anonymous Authors
Paper under double-blind review at ICLR 2027

Abstract · Method · Examples

Abstract

Continuous-strength instruction editing lets users specify what to change and by how much. Such methods provide this control for images, but remain underdeveloped for music because they require source-target examples or morph paths for supervision, which are hard to obtain. Image-based Kontinuous Kontext derives such trajectories from FreeMorph outputs, but requires post-filtering since interpolating zero-noise latents is non-smooth. We find image-based methods transfer poorly to music: they act like a volume slider and ignore musical traits like tempo and beat alignment. We therefore identify music morphing, not editor fine-tuning, as the upstream bottleneck. We introduce MusicMorpher, a music morphing method that generates instruction-conditioned morph paths between a source and target. We do so by learning a per-song style token, applying it via modulation rather than attention, and interpolating it continuously. At inference, we tempo- and beat-align songs before style-token computation to prevent rhythmic discontinuities. We compare MusicMorpher against SDEdit, RF-Inversion, FreeMorph, and FlowMorph on morph quality and smoothness, introducing trajectory-level measures absent from prior single-sample metrics. Results show our method produces higher-quality, smoother morphs, and, without supervised instruct-edit training, extends to continuous-strength semantic edits reaching quality-alignment trade-offs beyond baseline sweeps; our analyses explain prior methods' inconsistent results.

Method

MusicMorpher separates a song's style — its performance, instrumentation, and mix character — from its structure, and interpolates each independently. A single per-song style token is injected via AdaLN modulation and blended between two songs with spherical interpolation (slerp); each song's structural content is separately captured by inverting its latent to noise and slerp-interpolating the inverted noise trajectories. The two endpoints are also tempo- and beat-aligned before style-token computation, so the resulting morph path does not fight two competing rhythmic pulses. Full details, baselines, and quantitative comparisons are in the paper.

Examples

Each example below morphs between two source songs, Left and Right, each generated from the text prompt shown beneath it. We show the full morph path produced by MusicMorpher at eleven interpolation weights w, from w = 1 (Left) to w = 0 (Right), each with its mel-spectrogram and detected tempo so the transition can be seen and measured as well as heard. Quantitative comparisons against SDEdit, RF-Inversion, FreeMorph, and FlowMorph baselines are reported in the paper; this page focuses on qualitative listening examples for our method.
